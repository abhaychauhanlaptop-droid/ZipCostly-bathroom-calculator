## What drives the price of a bathroom remodel

**Size and scope.** A small hall bathroom costs far less than a primary suite, but cost doesn't scale perfectly with square feet. Every bathroom needs plumbing, a toilet, a vanity and a tub or shower, so small bathrooms cost more per square foot.

**Finish level.** Tile, the vanity and the tub or shower are where quality choices show up most. Porcelain tile from a big-box store and a stock vanity keep a remodel in the budget range. Natural stone, custom cabinetry and frameless glass push it to premium.

**Tub or walk-in shower.** Replacing a tub with a tiled walk-in shower usually costs more than a new tub with a tiled surround. The shower needs a waterproofed pan, more tile and a glass door or panel.

**Layout changes.** Moving the toilet, sink or shower means new drain and supply lines, opening floors and walls, and patching them afterward.

**Local labor rates.** Plumbers, electricians and tile setters earn very different wages from one metro area to the next. That is why the same remodel can cost thousands more in a high-cost city.

**Surprises behind the walls.** Rotted subfloor, old galvanized pipes or wiring that isn't up to code are common in older homes. Plan for a contingency of 10–20%.

## How to save on a bathroom remodel

- **Keep the layout.** Leaving fixtures where they are is the single biggest way to save.
- **Mix budget and premium.** Spend on what you touch every day, such as the faucet and shower valve, and save on wall tile and accessories.
- **Use large-format tile.** Fewer grout lines means less labor per square foot.
- **Refinish instead of replace.** A tub in good shape can often be reglazed for a few hundred dollars.
- **Buy your own fixtures, carefully.** Some contractors allow it, but confirm who handles returns and warranty problems.
- **Do the demolition yourself** only if your contractor agrees and you can do it safely.
- **Avoid peak season.** Contractors are often busiest in spring and summer.

## Questions to ask contractors

1. Are you licensed and insured in this state? Can I see the certificates?
2. Will you pull the permits, and are permit fees in the quote?
3. What exactly is included: demolition, disposal, materials, fixtures, paint, cleanup?
4. How do you waterproof the shower, and what warranty do you give on it?
5. Who does the plumbing and electrical work, and are they licensed?
6. What is the payment schedule? (Avoid paying a large share up front.)
7. How do you handle surprises like rotted subfloor, and how are change orders priced?
8. When can you start, and how long will the bathroom be out of service?
